# Designer — final review

## Initial impression and outcome

On first inspection, I landed partway down the page, below the revised header, and saw the feedback card still marked Open. The browser also showed “The request timed out. Your draft is preserved.” That initially left the revision uncertain.

After scrolling the shared phone preview to the top, I saw the updated 390 × 844 header: the brand occupies its own row, with Menu, Our story, and Visit presented as separate, larger buttons on a second row. This addresses my captured request for more readable, tappable navigation. I resolved the feedback once. The UI showed “Feedback resolved”; the conversation recorded “Resolved this feedback.” and the agent acknowledged, “Thanks, I’ve marked the phone navigation feedback as resolved.” The resolved feedback card remained visible. The timeout notice persisted, but it did not prevent the revision or resolution from being visible.

## Ratings

- **Clarity: 5/5.** The initial request named the phone header, described the visible tight row, and proposed a second row or compact menu. The agent replied with the specific change it would make and later described the full-width row and larger tap targets.
- **Collaboration: 5/5.** My captured annotation was attached to the “Visit” link, saved with a visible “Feedback saved” status, and later received a focused revision and a resolution acknowledgment.
- **Artifact review: 4/5.** I saw the revised header in the actual phone preview and confirmed the links now have their own row. Review covered the top viewport at 390 × 844, not the entire long page or a physical device.

## Remaining issues and limits

1. I did not navigate through each link or measure tap target dimensions; the revised buttons look more spacious in the phone preview.
2. I did not review lower sections after the header update, beyond the previously seen sample menu content.
3. The request-timeout banner remained visible during review even though the updated page, resolution status, and agent acknowledgment were all visible; the reason for that stale notice was not established.
